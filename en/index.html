Raw first.

Calotype is an iPhone camera. It shoots Apple ProRAW, saves the original in full first, then develops it into a photo on the phone and adds it to your photo library.

Join the TestFlight beta ↗

Beta build, installed through Apple’s TestFlight. Requires iOS 17 or later and an iPhone Pro model that supports Apple ProRAW.

01What it is

Shooting and developing are two steps

When you press the shutter, Calotype first writes the Apple ProRAW original into the app. Only then does it develop: it turns the original into a photo you can view and share right away (HEIF or JPEG) and saves it to your photo library.

The original stays in the app. If developing ever fails, you can develop it again from the original in the app’s album.

The name comes from the calotype, the process William Henry Fox Talbot patented in 1841: first a negative on paper, then a positive printed from it.

02How to use it

Four steps

  1. Frame

    The app opens straight to the rear camera viewfinder. Switch lenses (depending on your model), set exposure compensation, and long-press the viewfinder to lock exposure and focus.

  2. Shoot

    Press the shutter. The ProRAW original is saved in the app first.

  3. Develop

    Developing runs in a queue in the background. You don’t have to wait for it; keep shooting.

  4. Save

    Finished photos go to your photo library as HEIF, JPEG or both. In Settings you can also choose to save the ProRAW original alongside.

03Why

Three ideas behind it

Developed from RAW
Every photo is developed from its ProRAW original. The original stays in the app, so you can always go back to where you started.
Processed in the background
The shutter doesn’t wait for developing. Shooting and developing queue separately, so you can take the next shot right away.
A quiet interface
It opens to the viewfinder. The controls you use often stay at hand; the rest live in Settings.
